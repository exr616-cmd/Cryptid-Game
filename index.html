CHAPTER TWO — FATHER'S HOUSE

Area chapter · Investigation / Cryptid Research · Target: 8,500 words

THE MORNING AFTER

You didn't sleep.

You tried.

You lay on the sofa with the television on mute, staring at a news anchor whose mouth moved silently beneath a caption about the weather.

Every few minutes, you checked the clock.

Every time, it was a different time.

3:17.

4:02.

4:02.

4:03.

You eventually stopped looking.

The house was different in daylight.

Less threatening.

More familiar.

That somehow made it worse.

You made coffee in the kitchen and found yourself standing in the same place your father used to stand every morning.

You could picture him there.

Old mug.

Open newspaper.

One hand around the coffee.

The other making notes in the margins.

You remembered asking him what he was always writing.

"Things people forget."

You had thought he meant the news.

Now you weren't sure.

THE HOUSE AS EVIDENCE

You decided to investigate the house properly.

Not search.

Investigate.

There was a difference.

You opened your phone and created a new note.

FATHER'S HOUSE — INITIAL SEARCH

Then you started room by room.

The kitchen contained nothing unusual except a receipt from the diner dated three days before your father disappeared.

The garage contained camping equipment.

Two rifles.

A field camera.

Binoculars.

Thermal imaging equipment.

Three empty boxes marked WILDLIFE SURVEY.

One box marked NOCTURNE.

You opened it.

Inside were photographs.

Hundreds of them.

Most were ordinary.

Deer.

Foxes.

Birds.

Tracks.

Then the photographs changed.

A footprint in snow.

Fourteen inches long.

Too broad for a human foot.

A second photograph showed a line of prints disappearing into a forest.

The third showed the same prints ending abruptly.

You remembered the garden.

You put the photograph aside.

CASE FILE: BIGFOOT

The file contained reports from twenty-three years.

Most were garbage.

Blurry photographs.

Prank calls.

Tourists exaggerating what they saw.

Your father had marked them in different colours.

Green.

Yellow.

Red.

You opened the red files.

Those were different.

Multiple witnesses.

Independent reports.

Physical evidence.

One report described a figure crossing the road at 3:17 in the morning.

Another described footprints appearing in freshly fallen snow with no corresponding tracks entering the area.

A third described a low-frequency sound that caused several witnesses to vomit.

At the bottom of the page your father had written:

Not an animal.

You frowned.

Then beneath it:

Or not only an animal.

THE SECOND FILE

The Chupacabra file was thinner.

Mostly livestock reports.

Goats.

Sheep.

Chickens.

The strange thing was not the deaths.

It was the consistency.

No blood.

No obvious predator tracks.

No signs of feeding.

Your father had circled one sentence in a witness statement.

"The animal looked at me like it knew I was there."

Underneath it he had written:

Same as Blackwater.

You stopped.

Blackwater.

The forest west of town.

The place where you had seen something move between the trees.

You wrote it down.

THE GOATMAN

The next file contained almost no physical evidence.

That made it stranger.

People reported a figure beside roads.

A man.

Or something shaped like one.

Horns.

Long limbs.

Sometimes carrying an axe.

Sometimes simply standing there.

Your father had crossed out the word Goatman.

Beside it he had written:

Witness description only.

Then, underneath:

Don't assume the legend describes the thing.

You understood what he meant.

People saw something.

Then they gave it a name.

The name became the story.

The story became the expectation.

And eventually people stopped asking what they had actually seen.

THE FLATWOODS FILE

This one was old.

Very old.

The photograph on the front showed a group of people standing beside a hill.

Behind them was a shape.

Tall.

Dark.

Almost mechanical.

You turned the photograph over.

Your father's handwriting covered the back.

Everyone remembers the monster.

Almost nobody remembers the lights.

Underneath:

Why?

You looked back at the photograph.

The lights.

That was what mattered to him.

Not the creature.

The event surrounding it.

You began to understand the wall in the study.

He wasn't cataloguing monsters.

He was cataloguing encounters.

THE PATTERN

You spread the files across the floor.

Bigfoot.

Chupacabra.

Goatman.

Flatwoods.

Yeti.

Wendigo.

Skinwalker.

UFO.

Mothman.

Nine categories.

Hundreds of reports.

Thousands of miles.

But your father's notes kept pointing back to Nocturne County.

You opened the map.

The pins formed a shape.

Not a circle.

Not a straight line.

A spiral.

At the centre was the county.

At the outer edge were older sightings.

The newer the report, the closer it came to the centre.

You traced the line with your finger.

Bigfoot.

Blackwater Woods.

Goatman.

Old Station Road.

Chupacabra.

Farms outside town.

UFO sightings.

Nocturne Lake.

Mothman.

The cemetery.

You looked at the centre.

Your father's house.

You felt cold.

The house wasn't the centre of his investigation.

It was the centre of the pattern.

THE LOCKED DRAWER

There was one drawer you couldn't open.

You found it in the desk.

The lock was newer than the desk itself.

You searched the room for a key.

Nothing.

Then you remembered the key under the kitchen table.

You returned to the kitchen.

The tape was still there.

But the key was gone.

You stood completely still.

You were certain you'd left it on the desk.

You went back.

The key was there.

On top of the drawer.

You hadn't put it there.

You stared at it.

Then you unlocked the drawer.

Inside was a cassette recorder.

Three photographs.

A folded county map.

And a note.

You unfolded it.

If you're reading this, I failed to stop it.

You read it again.

Then the rest.

Do not trust the stories.

Do not trust the names.

Do not assume the creature is the danger.

The sightings are connected.

The lights are connected.

3:17 is connected.

And if you find the door, don't open it.

At the bottom:

I'm sorry.

THE RECORDING

You pressed play.

Static.

Your father's voice.

"Testing."

A pause.

"If you're hearing this, then I'm either missing or dead."

You stopped breathing.

"I need you to understand something."

Another pause.

"The creatures are real."

You stared at the recorder.

"Not necessarily in the way the stories describe them. That's the problem. People see something they can't explain, then they give it a shape. A name. A mythology."

Static.

"But underneath the stories there are patterns."

He sounded tired.

"I've spent years looking for those patterns."

A long silence.

"I think I finally found the source."

The recording crackled.

"If I am right, every sighting is a symptom of the same phenomenon."

Another pause.

"And the phenomenon is getting closer."

The tape distorted.

For a moment, your father's voice sounded impossibly deep.

Then normal again.

"If you come looking for me, start with Blackwater."

The recording ended.

You sat there.

Blackwater Woods.

Your father's first real lead.

You reached for the recorder.

The tape started playing again.

You hadn't pressed anything.

Static.

Then a different voice.

Very quiet.

"He's not there."

You froze.

"He's underneath."

The tape clicked off.

THE FIRST HUNT

You packed the field camera.

Binoculars.

Thermal imager.

Flashlight.

Your father's map.

The Bigfoot file.

You almost laughed at yourself.

You were actually doing it.

You were going into the woods to hunt Bigfoot.

Except your father hadn't called it hunting.

He called it verification.

You understood why now.

You weren't looking for a monster.

You were looking for evidence that something had happened.

Something your father had spent years trying to understand.

You stepped outside.

At the edge of the garden, you looked down.

The footprints from last night were gone.

Not washed away.

Gone.

Every trace had disappeared.

Except one.

A single footprint remained beside the front gate.

It pointed toward Blackwater Woods.

You followed it with your eyes.

Then you saw another.

Farther down the road.

Then another.

And another.

A trail.

Waiting.

You picked up your bag.

"Alright," you whispered.

"Let's see what Dad found."

Somewhere in the woods, something screamed.

It sounded almost human.

END OF CHAPTER TWO

CHAPTER THREE — BLACKWATER WOODS

Area chapter · First full cryptid hunt · Stealth / tactical combat / evidence

THE TRAIL

The footprints continued for almost two miles.

At first, following them felt ridiculous.

You had spent the morning telling yourself that you were investigating your father's work. That you were following evidence. That you were trying to understand what happened to him.

Now you were walking through Blackwater Woods with a thermal imager strapped to your chest and a twelve-inch footprint disappearing beneath your boots.

You checked the map.

Your father had marked a route through the western side of the forest.

The trail matched it almost perfectly.

That bothered you more than the footprints did.

He had known where they would lead.

The forest became quieter as you went deeper.

At the edge of the tree line, you could still hear the road.

Ten minutes later, there was nothing.

No traffic.

No birds.

No wind.

You stopped.

Silence.

You switched on the recorder.

"Blackwater Woods. Eleven forty-three. Following a track consistent with previous Nocturne reports."

You looked down.

The next footprint was fresh.

Rainwater hadn't collected in it.

You touched the edge.

Warm.

You pulled your hand away.

Something had passed through here recently.

HUNTING THE HUNTER

Your father's notes had included a checklist.

Don't approach immediately.

Observe first.

Establish wind direction.

Identify exits.

Never allow yourself to be surrounded.

You almost smiled.

Classic Dad.

You checked the wind.

East.

You moved west.

The first objective was simple:

Find the animal without letting it find you.

You crouched behind a fallen tree and raised the thermal imager.

Nothing.

More trees.

Rocks.

A fox.

You lowered it.

Then something moved.

Far away.

Between two trees.

You raised the thermal imager again.

A large heat signature.

Standing upright.

Too tall.

You zoomed in.

The figure turned.

You froze.

It had noticed you.

You lowered yourself behind the log.

Your breathing suddenly sounded enormous.

Branches snapped.

One.

Two.

Three.

The footsteps stopped.

You waited.

Nothing.

You slowly raised your head.

The figure was gone.

THE OBSERVATION SITE

You found your father's old camera position thirty minutes later.

A small platform hidden between the trees.

Camera mount.

Tripod marks.

Empty ammunition box being used as a seat.

And a notebook sealed inside a plastic bag.

You opened it.

Day 6.

It knows where the cameras are.

It doesn't destroy them.

It moves them.

You looked at the camera mount.

There was a fresh scratch across the metal.

Something had been here recently.

The notebook continued.

It avoids direct confrontation unless cornered.

It watches first.

Possible intelligence: high.

Then:

It is not afraid of me.

It is waiting for me.

You looked up.

Between the trees, something was watching.

You saw it for less than a second.

Dark fur.

Massive shoulders.

A face obscured by shadow.

Then it vanished.

You raised the camera.

Nothing.

THE STEALTH ENCOUNTER

You decided not to pursue it.

That was the first good decision you'd made all day.

You moved through the forest using the terrain for cover.

Fallen trees.

Rock formations.

Thick brush.

You marked the creature's last known position on the map.

Then you heard breathing.

Not yours.

Behind you.

You turned slowly.

Nothing.

The thermal imager showed a heat source directly behind the tree.

You backed away.

The heat source moved.

Fast.

You ran.

Branches hit your face.

You dropped behind a rock.

The creature passed through the clearing.

You saw it properly for the first time.

It was enormous.

Not twelve feet.

Not a movie monster.

Just large enough that your brain struggled to process its proportions.

Long arms.

Heavy torso.

Dark hair.

Human-like eyes.

It stopped.

You didn't move.

It sniffed the air.

Then it looked directly at you.

You had the sudden, horrible feeling that it understood exactly where you were.

You held your breath.

Five seconds.

Ten.

Twenty.

The creature turned away.

It walked into the trees.

You waited another minute before moving.

Your hands were shaking.

THE FIRST TACTICAL ENCOUNTER

A scream came from deeper in the woods.

Not the creature.

Human.

You ran toward it.

You found a man beside an overturned camera rig.

Blood ran down his arm.

"Don't shoot," he said.

You hadn't drawn your weapon.

"Who are you?"

"Daniel."

"What happened?"

He looked past you.

"It's here."

A tree cracked.

Daniel grabbed your sleeve.

"Don't let it see you."

You heard the creature moving through the forest.

Slowly.

Deliberately.

You had three options.

Run.

Hide.

Fight.

Your father's notebook had another instruction.

Never fight unless you have to.

You chose cover.

You pulled Daniel behind a fallen tree.

The creature entered the clearing.

It wasn't looking for Daniel.

It was looking for you.

You watched it through the gap between the roots.

It walked toward the overturned camera.

Stopped.

Picked it up.

Looked directly into the lens.

Then placed it upright.

It wasn't destroying the evidence.

It was posing for it.

Daniel whispered:

"What the hell is that?"

You didn't answer.

The creature suddenly turned toward you.

You moved.

The rifle came up.

You fired once into the ground beside it.

The creature recoiled.

Not from the bullet.

From the sound.

It screamed.

The noise hit you like pressure against your chest.

You stumbled.

Daniel fell beside you.

The creature charged.

You ran.

You didn't aim to kill it.

You aimed to survive.

You used the trees.

Hard left.

Down the slope.

Behind the rock.

Reload.

Wait.

Listen.

The creature slammed into the tree where you'd been standing.

You fired again.

This time it stopped.

It stared at you.

Then it looked past you.

At Daniel.

Its expression changed.

Fear.

It wasn't afraid of you.

It was afraid of something behind you.

You turned.

There was nothing.

When you looked back, the creature was gone.

THE EVIDENCE

You found the camera twenty minutes later.

It was still recording.

You watched the final footage.

The creature appeared.

It approached the camera.

Looked directly into the lens.

Then, behind it, the forest lights changed.

Three points of white light appeared between the trees.

The creature turned toward them.

It backed away.

Then it ran.

The lights remained.

They moved upward.

Slowly.

Silently.

Until they disappeared through the canopy.

You replayed the footage.

Again.

And again.

At the bottom of the final frame was a timestamp.

3:17

You stared at the screen.

Bigfoot hadn't been the end of the mystery.

It had been running from something.

THE HUNT CHANGES

Daniel sat against a tree while you bandaged his arm.

"You know what that thing was?" you asked.

He shook his head.

"Everyone calls it Bigfoot."

"Is that what it is?"

"I don't know."

"You've seen it before."

"Three times."

"And every time?"

"It runs."

"From what?"

Daniel looked toward the sky.

"The lights."

You showed him the photograph from your father's study.

He went pale.

"Where did you get that?"

"My father's house."

Daniel stood.

"Your father shouldn't have been looking into this."

"Why?"

"Because the last person who did disappeared."

"Who?"

Daniel looked at you.

"Your father."

You already knew that.

What you didn't know was what he said next.

"And the thing that took him wasn't Bigfoot."

CHAPTER DISCOVERY

You returned to the observation site before leaving the woods.

There was one final piece of evidence.

Scratched into the bark behind the camera mount were four words.

THEY ARE NOT THE MONSTERS.

Underneath was a symbol.

A circle.

Three vertical lines.

You photographed it.

Your phone screen flickered.

For a moment, the camera showed something that wasn't there.

A tall figure standing behind you.

You turned.

Nothing.

You looked back at the screen.

The figure was gone.

You packed the equipment.

As you left Blackwater Woods, you looked back one last time.

Two yellow eyes watched you from the darkness.

You raised your camera.

The eyes disappeared.

But the recorder caught a sound.

A low, almost human voice.

One word.

Run.

CHAPTER FOUR — MAIN STREET

Area chapter · Witness investigation · Chupacabra / Goatman leads · Social stealth

BACK IN TOWN

By the time you reached Main Street, the rain had returned.

Not the heavy kind. A thin drizzle that made the pavement shine beneath the streetlights.

You parked outside the diner. Daniel had refused to come with you. He had given you one piece of advice before disappearing into the trees.

"Don't tell people what you saw."

You hadn't. Not yet.

You walked inside. Mara was behind the counter.

"You went to Blackwater."

"How did you know?"

"Your father always came back looking like that."

You sat down.

"I found something."

"I figured."

"You knew he was investigating Blackwater."

Mara didn't answer.

"How long?"

"Your father came here almost every night for three months."

"Why?"

"He was talking to people."

"About what?"

"Animals."

"That's not what he was investigating."

"No."

She lowered her voice.

"He was investigating what people saw before the animals appeared."

You thought of the lights.

"Who should I talk to?"

Mara pointed toward the window.

"Gideon Pike."

GIDEON PIKE

Across the street, an old hardware store sat beneath a faded sign. Gideon Pike stood behind the counter.

"He came in here asking about goats," Gideon said.

"Chupacabra?"

"He didn't use the word."

"What did he ask?"

"Whether I'd seen animals drained of blood."

"Have you?"

"Yes."

Six months earlier, seven goats had died at Miller Farm. No predator tracks. No obvious bite marks. No blood.

Gideon produced a photograph. It showed the dead animals in a field and, far behind them, a pale shape.

"What's that?"

"Don't know."

"You saw it?"

"I saw something."

"What happened next?"

"The lights appeared."

"Three of them?"

Gideon stared at you.

"You've seen them."

You didn't answer.

"Your father said you would."

THE FARM

Miller Farm was twenty minutes outside town. You photographed the field and found three parallel scratches in the soil: the same symbol from your father's study.

A noise came from the barn.

You approached with your flashlight low. Something moved above you. Two eyes reflected the beam.

A shape dropped from the rafters, hit the floor and vanished through a gap in the boards.

You checked the thermal imager. A heat signature moved beneath the barn.

You followed it into an old tunnel.

THE CHUPACABRA

The chamber contained goat bones and dozens of photographs — your father's photographs.

The creature appeared at the far end.

It was smaller than you expected. Four-legged. Thin. Hairless. Its mouth was full of small teeth.

It stared at you.

You raised the camera.

Flash.

It recoiled, then ran.

You followed, but didn't shoot. The creature vanished through a gap too small for you.

It left something behind: a strip of dark material caught on a nail.

It wasn't fur.

It was skin.

Human skin.

THE GOATMAN CASE

Back in town, a mechanic named Ellis told you about a figure beside the old bridge.

"A man," he said.

"What was wrong with him?"

"His legs. They bent wrong."

Horns. Long limbs. A knife. But it never attacked Ellis. It followed him for three days.

Then Ellis saw the lights.

You went to the bridge that evening.

A thermal signature appeared on the other side: human-shaped, with two horns.

You stayed hidden.

The figure walked toward you, stopped, tilted its head and looked directly at your hiding place.

You raised the camera.

Click.

Three lights appeared above the trees.

The Goatman looked up and ran.

Your recorder crackled.

A voice whispered through the static.

"Two."

You checked the recording. The voice repeated itself.

"Two."

You looked at the bridge.

There was a second set of footprints beside yours. They had appeared while you were standing there.

MARA'S SECRET

You returned to the diner. Mara admitted your father had asked her to watch the house the night before he disappeared.

"Why didn't you tell me?"

"Because I was hoping you'd leave."

She handed you an envelope addressed in your father's handwriting.

FOR MARA — IF HE COMES BACK

Inside was a photograph of St. Mercy Cemetery.

On the back:

The next sighting will be at the cemetery.

Don't follow the creature.

Follow what it's afraid of.

Mara looked at you.

"What's at the cemetery?"

"Something your father was terrified of."

CHAPTER DISCOVERY

You stepped outside. Your phone vibrated.

A new photograph had appeared in your camera roll. You hadn't taken it.

It showed the cemetery. Three lights above the trees. And beneath them, a tall figure with wings.

Mothman.

You zoomed in. The figure wasn't looking at the camera.

It was looking at something behind it.

You turned.

Nothing.

Then your phone displayed one final line.

3:17 — ST. MERCY CEMETERY

You closed the phone.

For the first time, you understood your father's method.

Don't chase the monster. Follow the pattern.

V103 — CRYPTID ECOLOGY

Lesser cryptids are now a persistent part of Nocturne County's world. They populate specific habitats, appear under particular conditions, and serve as common enemies during missions without replacing the major cryptids as the game's principal investigations.

PALE CRAWLER

Areas: caves, mines, ravines, abandoned tunnels.

Role: ambush predator.

Behaviour: stays low, hides in darkness, attacks from blind spots, retreats when exposed.

Gameplay: stealth and flashlight management are important. Its preferred tactic is forcing the player out of cover.

LITTLE GREY

Areas: UFO sites, crop fields, observatories, electrical facilities.

Role: ranged/scouting enemy.

Behaviour: observes before engaging and can signal nearby entities.

Gameplay: cover, line-of-sight and electronic interference become important.

JUVENILE SASQUATCH

Areas: Blackwater Woods, mountain trails and deep forest.

Role: mobile melee enemy.

Behaviour: territorial, fast, intelligent enough to retreat and reposition.

Gameplay: teaches tracking, cover and controlled tactical engagements.

CHUPACABRA PUP

Areas: farms, barns, livestock pens and rural outskirts.

Role: pack enemy.

Behaviour: circles targets, coordinates attacks and attempts to isolate stragglers.

Gameplay: weak individually but dangerous in numbers.

DOG SKINWALKER

Areas: rural roads, forests, abandoned properties.

Role: stealth/tracking enemy.

Behaviour: imitates familiar sounds and uses pursuit rather than immediate aggression.

Gameplay: audio cues can be misleading; players must verify what they hear.

MOTHLET

Areas: cemeteries, abandoned buildings, wooded clearings.

Role: swarm/disruption enemy.

Behaviour: gathers around light and reacts strongly to electronics.

Gameplay: obscures vision, disrupts equipment and can expose a stealthy player.

AREA ECOLOGY

Each area receives a weighted lesser-cryptid encounter pool rather than a universal enemy list. The pool can change with time, weather, story progress and supernatural conditions.

  • Blackwater Woods: Juvenile Sasquatch, Pale Crawlers, later Dog Skinwalkers. Major association: Bigfoot.
  • Miller Farm / rural outskirts: Chupacabra Pups, Dog Skinwalkers. Major association: Chupacabra.
  • St. Mercy Cemetery: Mothlets and nocturnal variants. Major association: Mothman.
  • Mountain regions: Pale Crawlers, Juvenile Sasquatch and cold-adapted variants. Major association: Yeti.
  • UFO sites: Little Greys and anomalous drones. Major association: UFO phenomena.
  • Deep corrupted forest: Dog Skinwalkers, Pale Crawlers and progressively more dangerous manifestations. Major association: Wendigo / Skinwalker phenomena.

ENCOUNTER RULES

Lesser cryptids are not simply disposable combat units. A player can evade them, photograph them, track them, investigate their habitat, collect biological evidence, lure them, or fight them. Certain encounters reward observation over violence.

Major cryptids remain distinct. Seeing a Juvenile Sasquatch does not mean the player has found Bigfoot. Finding Chupacabra Pups does not complete the Chupacabra investigation. Lesser encounters instead provide evidence, environmental storytelling, resources and clues that can feed into major investigations.

THE ECOLOGY LOOP

Encounter → observe → identify → document → learn behaviour → exploit habitat → survive or capture evidence → update Cryptid Journal → unlock deeper investigation.

The player gradually learns that Nocturne County has an ecosystem of anomalous creatures. The deeper mystery begins when multiple species display the same unusual behaviour: fear of the three lights, unexplained movement toward or away from specific areas, and activity around 3:17.

Design rule: The player should be able to become better at surviving cryptids through knowledge, not merely stronger through equipment.

CHAPTER FIFTEEN — THE END OF NOCTURNE

Final chapter · Ten endings · Character resolution · True Ending

DAWN

The forest was quiet.

For the first time in years, you could hear birdsong without wondering what was hiding beneath it.

Your father was alive.

Mara was beside you.

The county was still standing.

But none of you knew what had changed.

Your father looked toward the trees.

"It isn't over."

You stood.

"Then we finish it."

He smiled.

"No."

He looked at you.

"You decide what comes next."

THE COUNTY REMEMBERS

Over the following hours, the places you visited began to settle.

The quarry stopped glowing.

The strange lights disappeared.

The forest markings faded.

The observatory went dark.

Some cryptids vanished.

Others remained.

And some things were never explained.

YOUR LAST INVESTIGATION

The final state of Nocturne depends on what you discovered, who you trusted, which creatures you spared or killed, what memories you recovered, and what you chose beneath the county.

There is no single history of Nocturne.

There is only the history you created.

ENDING 1 — THE ANCHOR

You take your father's place.

The door closes around you.

Your father returns to the county.

Mara stays with him.

For years, people occasionally report a figure standing beneath the northern lights.

They say it watches the county.

They say it keeps the door shut.

Sometimes, when Mara visits the woods, she leaves flowers at the boundary.

She never calls you dead.

She calls you the person who stayed.

ENDING 2 — THE SURVIVOR

You leave your father behind.

The door closes.

You return home.

Life continues.

Your father does not.

You tell yourself that surviving was the only choice you had.

Years later, you still dream of the inverted county.

Sometimes you wake to find dirt beneath your fingernails.

ENDING 3 — THE HUNTER

You reject the boundary and dedicate your life to hunting every cryptid you can find.

You become a legend among hunters.

The county becomes quieter.

But the silence isn't peace.

It's absence.

By the end, you have killed many creatures that were never your enemy.

And somewhere beyond the boundary, something learns from every death.

ENDING 4 — THE WATCHER

You spare the cryptids and remain in Nocturne County.

You become its protector.

Mara helps you establish a network of safe routes and observation posts.

Your father leaves to investigate other boundaries.

You don't stop the strange things from happening.

You simply make sure someone is there when they do.

ENDING 5 — THE EXILE

You leave Nocturne County with Mara.

Neither of you talks about what happened for a long time.

You start over somewhere far away.

Normal life slowly returns.

Then, one winter night, you see a red light outside your window.

Mara sees it too.

Neither of you says anything.

ENDING 6 — THE FALSE DAWN

You accept the entity's perfect world.

Your father is home.

Mara never lied.

The county is ordinary.

There are no monsters.

There are no missing people.

There is no door.

Everything is perfect.

Except you remember choosing it.

And every morning, at exactly 3:17, the lights flicker.

ENDING 7 — THE BROKEN LOCK

You break the door without understanding the full pattern.

The boundary collapses.

The county survives.

But the worlds bleed together.

People begin seeing impossible roads.

Animals develop unfamiliar tracks.

The sky occasionally turns black at noon.

You saved your father.

You may have endangered everyone else.

ENDING 8 — THE FORGOTTEN CHILD

You fail to recover enough of your childhood memories.

The entity takes advantage of the gaps.

You leave the county alive, but you cannot remember why you came.

Mara remembers.

Your father remembers.

You don't.

Years later, someone asks you about Nocturne County.

You answer honestly:

"I've never heard of it."

ENDING 9 — THE LAST LIGHT

You uncover the truth but refuse both sacrifice and surrender.

You close the boundary and leave your father on the other side.

Mara stays with you.

The cryptids disappear.

The county heals.

But one light remains above the trees.

Mothman.

Watching.

Not threatening.

Waiting.

ENDING 10 — NOCTURNE

You break the door.

But instead of destroying the boundary, you understand it.

The six marks were never keys.

The seventh was never a lock.

They were coordinates.

A map between realities.

You realise the entity's greatest lie.

It told you that someone had to remain behind.

That was never true.

The boundary could be crossed both ways.

You reach for your father.

Mara reaches for you.

The cryptids gather around the boundary.

Bigfoot stands beneath the trees.

Mothman watches from above.

The lights form a circle in the sky.

The entity attacks.

You don't fight it with hatred.

You don't become its monster.

You remember.

You choose.

You refuse.

The entity collapses inward.

The door becomes a doorway.

And then it becomes nothing.

THE CHILD

You are six years old again.

Your father kneels in front of you.

Mara is standing behind him.

The forest is quiet.

Your father holds out his hand.

"Come on."

You take it.

The memory changes.

You are twenty-three years older.

You are standing beside him.

He looks at you.

"I'm sorry."

You look at him.

"I know."

He starts crying.

So do you.

FORGIVENESS

Your father didn't disappear because he didn't love you.

He disappeared because he believed disappearing was the only way to protect you.

It was a mistake.

But it was a mistake made from love.

You forgive him.

Not because what happened didn't matter.

Because it did.

THE COUNTY

Months pass.

Nocturne changes.

The strange lights become rare.

The disappearances stop.

The cryptids retreat deeper into the wilderness.

Researchers arrive.

Hunters arrive.

People tell stories.

You stay.

Your father stays too.

Mara opens the old ranger station again.

THE NEW LIFE

One evening, you sit outside the station with your father.

Mara brings three cups of coffee.

Your father laughs.

"Still terrible."

"You still drink it."

"Some things don't change."

You look toward the forest.

For a moment, you see two red eyes.

Then they disappear.

Your father notices.

"You saw it?"

You smile.

"Yeah."

"Scared?"

You look at Mara.

Then at your father.

"No."

THE FINAL IMAGE

The camera pulls away from the three of you.

Past the station.

Past the forest.

Past the county.

Far above Nocturne, a single red light moves across the stars.

It stops.

For one second, another light appears beside it.

Then both disappear.

The screen goes black.

THE END.
